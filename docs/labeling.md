# Maneuver Labeling Methodology

This document describes how sequence-level maneuver labels are derived for the
DATASCI 281 Group 2 final project. Labels are generated by
`scripts/build_train_manifest.py` and stored in `data/train_manifest.json`.

## Overview

The task is 5-class maneuver prediction from ego-vehicle camera imagery. Each
sequence is assigned exactly one of five maneuver labels:

- `straight`
- `left-turn`
- `right-turn`
- `lane-change-left`
- `lane-change-right`

Labels are derived from the geometry of the ego-vehicle's **future trajectory**,
not from camera imagery and not from Waymo's native routing intent. A sixth
transient category, `stationary`, is produced by the classifier but is rare
(~0.5% of sequences) and reflects clips with negligible motion.

Labeling operates at the sequence level: each sequence receives a single label,
determined by the most complex maneuver observed across its constituent frames
(see [Sequence-Level Assignment](#sequence-level-assignment)).

## Dataset

Labels are computed over the Waymo Open Dataset End-to-End (WOD-E2E) training
split [1]. After preprocessing with the StandardE2E pipeline, the training split
comprises 415,663 per-frame `.npz` files spanning **2,037 sequences**. Each frame
carries a future trajectory of 20 waypoints sampled at 4 Hz (a 5-second horizon),
expressed in an ego-relative coordinate frame with the origin at the current
vehicle position, +x forward and +y to the left.

The resulting label distribution is:

| Maneuver | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| Straight | 745 | 36.6% |
| Right-turn | 467 | 22.9% |
| Left-turn | 392 | 19.2% |
| Lane-change-right | 222 | 10.9% |
| Lane-change-left | 200 | 9.8% |
| Stationary | 11 | 0.5% |

## Why trajectory geometry, not Waymo intent

The WOD-E2E data includes a per-sequence `intent` field with values
`GO_STRAIGHT`, `GO_LEFT`, and `GO_RIGHT`. These are high-level routing commands,
not maneuver labels. A cross-tabulation of intent against derived maneuvers
(see `notebooks/label_design.ipynb`) shows that each intent value spreads across
multiple maneuvers: `GO_STRAIGHT` sequences include substantial turning and
lane-change behavior, and lane changes have no dedicated intent value at all.
Intent describes where the vehicle is headed on the road network, not the
maneuver executed within the observation window. We therefore derive our own
maneuver labels from trajectory geometry, using intent only as a comparison
baseline.

## Classification method

Maneuver classification follows the Waymo Open Motion Dataset's official
trajectory-shape classifier, `ClassifyTrack` [2], ported to operate on the
WOD-E2E future trajectory. The classifier assigns a maneuver based on the net
displacement and net heading change between the first and last valid points of
the future trajectory.

### Thresholds

The thresholds are adopted directly from Waymo's `ClassifyTrack`
implementation [2]:

| Parameter | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Max speed for stationary | 2.0 m/s | Below this (with small displacement) → stationary |
| Max displacement for stationary | 3.0 m | Below this (with low speed) → stationary |
| Max heading change for straight | 30° (π/6 rad) | Below this → straight or lane-change; above → turn |
| Max lateral displacement for straight | 2.5 m | Distinguishes straight from lane-change when heading change is small |

### Decision logic

1. **Stationary** if maximum speed < 2.0 m/s and net displacement < 3.0 m.
2. Otherwise, compute the net heading change between start and end.
   - If |heading change| < 30°: the maneuver is **straight** if lateral
     displacement < 2.5 m, else a **lane-change** in the direction of the
     lateral displacement.
   - If |heading change| ≥ 30°: the maneuver is a **turn** in the direction of
     the lateral displacement.

Turn and lane-change direction is taken from the sign of net lateral
displacement in the ego-relative frame (+y left, −y right).

### Adaptations from the original

Two adaptations were required relative to Waymo's `ClassifyTrack`:

1. **Reconstructed heading and speed.** Waymo's implementation reads a recorded
   `heading` field and per-state `velocity`. The WOD-E2E future states do not
   populate these components (only X, Y, Z, timestamp, and a validity flag are
   provided). Heading is therefore reconstructed from the trajectory tangent and
   speed from consecutive positions divided by the timestamp interval. To avoid
   ill-defined headings on near-stationary segments, headings are computed only
   from trajectory segments that span at least 0.5 m of displacement.

2. **U-turn collapse.** Waymo's taxonomy distinguishes `LEFT_U_TURN` and
   `RIGHT_U_TURN` from ordinary turns. This task has no u-turn class, and the
   short forward-driving WOD-E2E clips do not contain genuine u-turns. U-turn
   outcomes are therefore collapsed into their corresponding turn direction.

## Sequence-Level Assignment

Each sequence contains many frames, each with its own future trajectory and
therefore its own frame-level maneuver classification. The sequence label is the
highest-ranked maneuver observed across its frames, under the ranking

```
turn (5) > lane-change (4) > straight (3) > stationary (1)
```

This favors the most complex maneuver present, so a sequence containing a turn is
labeled as a turn even if most of its frames are straight.

## Target Frame and Context Window

For each sequence, the manifest records a **target frame** used for
classification and a **context window** of preceding frames for optional
multi-frame (image-difference) features.

- **Target frame:** the frame immediately preceding the highest-ranked maneuver
  frame. When the highest-ranked frame is the first in the sequence, the target
  is shifted forward to the second frame so that at least one preceding frame is
  always available as context.
- **Context window:** up to 20 frames preceding and including the target frame,
  stored as `context_fnames`, with the count recorded as `n_context`.

Across the 2,037 sequences, context window sizes range from 1 to 20 frames
(mean 12.0). Five sequences have `n_context = 1` (target frame only); these
cannot support a frame-difference feature and are handled as an edge case by the
feature pipeline.

## Manifest Schema

Each entry in `data/train_manifest.json` is keyed by sequence ID:

```json
"<sequence_id>": {
  "label": "straight",
  "target_fname": "<sequence_id>_<frame>.npz",
  "context_fnames": ["<sequence_id>_<frame>.npz", ...],
  "n_context": 12,
  "best_fname": "<sequence_id>_<frame>.npz"
}
```

- `label` — the sequence-level maneuver label.
- `target_fname` — the frame used for feature extraction.
- `context_fnames` — preceding frames (including target) for multi-frame features.
- `n_context` — number of context frames.
- `best_fname` — the highest-ranked maneuver frame the label was derived from.

## Design Notes

The classifier described here replaced an earlier approach that estimated turn
direction from the angle between the first and second halves of the trajectory.
That method was unstable on trajectories containing stationary segments (for
example, braking to a stop or starting from rest): a stationary half has no
well-defined direction, and the angle between halves degenerated to ±180°,
misclassifying straight-line motion as turns. Adopting Waymo's net-displacement
and net-heading-change formulation eliminates this failure mode by construction,
because a stationary segment contributes near-zero net displacement rather than a
noisy direction estimate. The 0.5 m displacement gate on heading reconstruction
provides additional robustness for the reconstructed-heading case.
